Zoltan remarks:

output gap parameter in the policy rule should actually be 0, since the forward looking inflation term captures its original role as a proxy for inflation expectations (the great Hungarian mathematician Paul Erdős wrote his letters in such a cut-to-the-chase style ... :))

But really, I think any value other than 0 is either a misunderstanding of the original Taylor rule (with contemporaneous inflation and output gap proxying for inflationary pressures), or an improper representation of a very dovish monetary policy, which would never fight inflation when the economy is in recession, in which case it wouldn't have to anyway, since the recession automatically will bring inflation back to target (not to mention that the output gap term will lead to excessively hawkish reaction when the economy is booming). If dovish policy is the reason to have the output gap in the policy rule, than a much simpler and more consistent solution is to simply lower the reaction parameter, which is already very low for us at 1.2.
